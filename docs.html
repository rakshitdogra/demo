Platform blueprint

Turn a citizen's problem into a deployed, funded solution.

A state-level pipeline that takes a problem reported by any citizen, routes it through AI triage and government validation, matches it to the university best equipped to research it, and pulls industry in to prototype and deploy the result.

Positioning

Not a grievance portal wearing a new name.

Grievance systems end at "resolved." This pipeline treats a reported problem as a research brief — something a university team can pick up, an industry partner can fund, and a district can point to as a deployed fix.

Ecosystem

Five roles, one shared pipeline.

Every role sees a different dashboard, but every action moves the same underlying record forward.

Citizen
Reports a problem, tracks it end to end.
Government
Validates, prioritises, monitors districts.
University / HEI
Accepts the challenge, runs student teams.
Industry
Mentors, funds, prototypes, deploys.
Admin
Runs the ecosystem, quality and escalation.
The core mechanic

The challenge lifecycle is the product.

Nearly everything else — dashboards, matching, notifications — exists to move a single challenge through these ten stages.

1
Submit
A citizen describes a problem in plain language, with photos, video or a voice note.
2
AI pre-processing
Category, severity and duplicate signals are generated before a human sees it.
3
Expert validation
A government reviewer confirms it's real, non-duplicate, and worth publishing.
4
Published & matched
The challenge goes live and is ranked against every university's capability profile.
5
HEI accepts
A university claims the challenge and forms a faculty-led student team.
6
Solution proposed
The team turns the brief into a proposal and requests industry input.
7
Industry matched
A partner offers funding, hardware, or manufacturing support.
8
Prototype & pilot
The solution is built and tested in the field, in the district that raised it.
9
Deployment
The piloted fix is rolled out beyond the original village or ward.
10
Impact measured
People benefited, cost saved and time-to-resolution recorded against the original report.
Entry point

Ask nothing technical on the citizen side.

Never ask someone to "describe the technological intervention required." Ask what's wrong, in their own words — the platform translates it into a structured brief afterwards.

Captured per report: title, description, category, exact location, evidence (photo / video / voice), who's affected, and how often it happens.

Access, not just translation

A problem shouldn't go unheard because of the language it's told in.

The pitch, stated plainly

Don't describe it as a place to file complaints.

There are already enough of those. What makes this worth building is the second half of the sentence.

WEAKER FRAME

"A portal where citizens submit complaints."

STRONGER FRAME

"An AI-enabled pipeline that turns community problems into university-led research and industry-backed, deployable solutions."

A demo of one report — from a citizen's photo to a funded prototype — carries almost the entire concept.
